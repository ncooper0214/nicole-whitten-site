The challenge

HUMAN Security had grown into a leader in protecting businesses from bots and digital fraud, but its brand and website still told an older, narrower story. The messaging leaned heavily on technical detail, the voice shifted from page to page, and visitors had to work harder than they should to understand why HUMAN mattered to them.

HUMAN partnered with Baunfire, a digital agency focused on technology brands, for a full overhaul of the brand and site, and the words needed to lead it. The new content had to speak to technical buyers and executive decision-makers alike, move in step with a new visual identity, and give HUMAN's own team a voice they could carry forward long after launch.

My role and leadership

As Content Strategy Director at Baunfire, I led the content strategy and wrote multiple pages of the website copy myself, including the homepage and product pages. Baunfire's work with technology companies meant we could speak HUMAN's language from day one, and I worked closely with HUMAN's marketing leadership, writers, and designers, alongside our own designers and content strategists, so that copy, design, and identity came together as a single, unified experience.

  • Developed the content strategy that shaped the new site's structure and messaging
  • Wrote the website copy myself, from the homepage headline through every product page
  • Defined the voice and messaging so every page sounded like one confident brand
  • Kept copy, design, and brand identity aligned across two teams
The insight

From what the technology does to what it protects.

HUMAN had been leading with how its technology worked, but our conversations kept returning to what it made possible: trust between businesses and the real people they serve. Shifting the story from capability to consequence gave the brand a clearer, warmer point of view, and that idea became the throughline for the entire site.

The approach

01
Ask better questions

Through stakeholder interviews and working sessions, I helped HUMAN's team put words to ideas they had been circling but hadn't yet articulated.

02
Build the strategy

I turned those insights into a content strategy that defined the audiences, messaging priorities, and page structure for the new site.

03
Find the voice

I defined how HUMAN should sound, confident and clear without leaning on jargon, and built that voice into the messaging every page would draw from.

04
Write the site

With the strategy and voice in place, I wrote multiple pages of the site copy myself, working in step with design so words and visuals were built together rather than fitted together at the end.

Navigating the hard parts

Technical depth versus clarity

Security buyers expect substance, while executives want the point quickly. I layered the messaging so each page led with clear value and let the technical proof follow for the readers who wanted it.

Many voices, one brand

With stakeholders and writers on both teams, I used the content strategy and messaging framework as a shared reference point, which kept feedback focused and decisions consistent.

Copy and design in step

Rather than handing off copy at the end, I worked alongside the designers throughout, so the messaging shaped the layouts and the layouts sharpened the messaging.

What I learned

Good messaging starts before the first headline.

The most valuable part of this project happened in the conversations that helped HUMAN's team see their own story more clearly. Once that story was clear, the copy had something true to say, and because I wrote the pages myself, that story carried through the whole site without losing anything in the handoff.

I bring that same curiosity to every brand project now, because clear messaging almost always begins with a question no one has thought to ask yet.
